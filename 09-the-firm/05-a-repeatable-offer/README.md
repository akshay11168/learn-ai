# 05 — A repeatable offer

Prerequisite: [the consulting packet](../../08-consulting/06-rehearse-an-engagement/README.md) and [01 When to form the firm](../01-when-to-form-the-firm/README.md).

## Purpose

Turn the wedge into one offer you can deliver a second time without inventing the process again. Firms that stay alive have a repeated motion: the same shape of pilot, the same artifacts, the same limits. Custom work still happens inside that shape.

## Explain before you code

1. Why is "we do whatever the client asks with AI" harder to staff, price, and insure than one pilot shape?
2. What do you keep in a private template, and what do you rewrite for each client?
3. How will a stranger know the offer is for them, in one sentence, without a model name?

## The offer

Write `offer.md`.

- **Name.** Plain words. "Support-ticket triage pilot" beats a coined brand.
- **Who it is for, and who it is not for.** Reuse the nos from discovery.
- **What they get.** The artifact list from the SOW: problem statement, baseline, system, golden-set report, runbook, readout.
- **How long and how they participate.** Their reviewer's hours, not only yours.
- **Price range.** From the pricing lab, as a range, with the sentence that the SOW fixes the number after discovery.
- **What week 1 and the last week look like.** So you can describe the work without a slide.
- **What you will have learned from the first delivery that makes the second faster.** Name the templates: discovery questions, eval script, handover skeleton.

**Operations beside the offer,** short on purpose:

- A pipeline of conversations: who you will ask for a discovery, and a reminder that one rehearsal does not entitle you to spam strangers. Warm introductions and one clear paragraph are the standard to practice.
- A capacity rule: how many pilots you can run at once on this laptop and your evenings. The honest number at the start is one.
- A name and a basic site or a single PDF are optional. They come after the offer text is stable. The PDF is the case study plus the offer, with the self-run pilot labeled honestly.
- A simple record of leads: date, person, problem, next step, and whether you declined. A spreadsheet is enough. This is how you notice that every lead is outside the wedge.

## You are done when

You can describe the offer in two minutes, the price is a range you can derive, and the capacity rule says how many you will not take.
