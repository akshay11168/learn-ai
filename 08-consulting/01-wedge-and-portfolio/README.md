# 01 — Wedge and portfolio

Prerequisite: [path 04 lab 05](../../04-small-use-cases/05-ship-one-small-tool/README.md) and [path 07 lab 01](../../07-production-practice/01-the-smallest-system/README.md). A second finished use case makes the portfolio stronger. One is enough to draft the wedge.

## Purpose

Choose a narrow kind of problem you will be known for, and write the proof from work you have done. A general "AI consultant" competes with everyone who can open a chat window. A wedge is a class of workflows you understand well enough to say no to the others.

## Explain before you code

1. Why does a buyer trust a specific story ("we cut the time to tag incoming requests") more than a list of model names?
2. Which of your labs is a real workflow, and which is only a technique demo? A portfolio piece has a user, a baseline, and a measured result.
3. What work will you decline for the next year so the wedge stays true?

## Build

**Wedge.** One sentence in `wedge.md`: who it is for, the job you improve, and the method family you actually use (for example retrieval over their documents, a classifier on their tickets, a vision check on their photos). Add three problems that fit and three that do not. The ones that do not should include something tempting and fashionable that you are not ready to deliver.

You may revise the sentence after path 05 and after the first real conversations. Date the revision. Do not collect five wedges.

**Case studies.** Two documents, one to two pages each, from your own projects. Structure:

- The situation, in plain language.
- What you refused to build, and the baseline you compared against.
- What you built, including the rung on the ladder.
- The number: metric, data size, split, and the limit of the test.
- What broke, from the error tags.
- What a client would still need (their data, their reviewer, their hosting).

If nobody external has used it, the first line says this was a self-run pilot on your own data. That sentence is part of the professional standard. Hiring managers and clients can tell a fabricated logo from a careful personal project. The careful one is the asset.

Store the studies here. Link the handover packet from path 07 when it exists.

## Study this step

**Concepts to master**

- A wedge is a who, a job, and a method family, plus an explicit list of work you will not take.
- A case study is a measurement story: baseline, system, number, limit. A screenshot without a baseline is an advertisement.
- Self-run pilots are labeled as self-run. False client logos are a trust failure you do not get back.
- Portfolio pieces come from path 04 and path 07, not from a new imaginary project.

**Study**

- Patrick McKenzie, "Don't Call Yourself A Programmer, And Other Career Advice": https://www.kalzumeus.com/2011/10/28/dont-call-yourself-a-programmer/ — read the parts on selling outcomes rather than tools. Translate "programmer" to "person who fine-tunes models."
- Google's Rules of ML, rule 1, again, as the spine of a case study: the heuristic and the baseline stay visible. https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml
- One case study you admire from a consultancy or a research write-up that shows a negative result. If you cannot find one, that absence is the lesson. Your studies will include the failure tags.

**Practice**

- Say the wedge in twenty seconds to a recording. Cut any model name that is not the method family.
- Draft both case studies with the number left blank, then fill the number only from the log. If the log has no number, the study waits.
- List three tempting projects you are refusing for a year.

**Practice questions**

1. Why is "AI consulting for enterprises" not a wedge?
2. Your study says the model is "very accurate." Replace that sentence with a metric, a baseline, and a limit.
3. When must the first line of the study say the pilot was self-run?
4. Which of your labs is not allowed to become a case study yet, and why?
5. A buyer asks if you can also do real-time video analytics. What does your "not this year" list tell you to say?

## You are done when

You can say the wedge aloud in twenty seconds, and each case study contains a baseline and a number you can point to in the experiment log.
