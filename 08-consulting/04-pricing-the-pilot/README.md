# 04 — Pricing the pilot

Prerequisite: [03 Proposal and statement of work](../03-proposal-and-statement-of-work/README.md).

## Purpose

Put a price on the SOW by counting time, and choose a commercial shape that a first client can say yes to. The number will change with your city and your experience. The method should not: know the hours, know what is extra, and do not hide risk inside a number you cannot explain.

## Explain before you code

1. Why is a price with no hour estimate a guess you cannot defend when the data is messier than discovery showed?
2. What is the failure mode of billing by the hour with no cap, for a client who has never bought this work?
3. Why should a pilot be priced as a pilot, instead of as a discount on a large transformation?

## A way to build the number

In `pricing.md`, make a table for the SOW you wrote:

| Activity | Hours you believe | Hours if the data is messy |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery cleanup and labeling guide | | |
| Baseline and error review | | |
| Build the system you scoped | | |
| Golden set and report | | |
| Handover and two meetings | | |

Add a contingency row of about 20% for the first real pilots, because your estimates will be low. Multiply by a day rate you choose and write down how you chose it (a target income, local contracting rates you have actually seen, or a placeholder you will replace before sending). Convert hours to days at a number you can keep (for example six hours of client-billable work in a day, because the rest is unpaid admin). The result is the price of the pilot.

Commercial shape for the first engagements:

- **Fixed price for a fixed SOW.** The client knows the spend. You carry the risk if you estimated badly, which is why the exclusions and the change rule exist. This is the right default for a small pilot.
- **Time and materials with a cap.** Useful when discovery showed the data is unknown. The cap is what makes it safe for them.
- **A retainer.** A monthly amount for a named number of days, after a pilot has succeeded. Do not lead with a retainer.

Write the payment shape in plain language: a portion to start, a portion at the midpoint review, a portion when the artifacts are delivered. You are not giving invoicing or tax advice. You are deciding the milestones so the SOW and the money line up. A chartered accountant tells you how to invoice in your country.

Also write what you will not do for free: an open-ended "quick look" at their whole archive, a second workflow, or production babysitting. A short paid discovery is allowed if the problem statement cannot be written without it. That discovery is its own small fixed price, credited against the pilot if they continue.

## Verify

You can explain the price as hours times rate, point at the contingency, and say which request would trigger the change rule instead of silent extra work.

## You are done when

`pricing.md` has a number for your practice SOW, a messy-data range, and a sentence you could say to a client without apologizing for charging.
