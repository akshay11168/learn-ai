# 08 — Consulting

Consulting is paid responsibility for a client's outcome, not a job title you add because you finished a course. The technical paths are what make the advice true. This path is the commercial craft around them: who you help, how you learn their problem, what you put in writing, what you charge, and how you behave when the model is wrong.

You can practice every lab here before anyone pays you. Use your own use case as the stand-in client. Do not invent a company, a metric, or a logo and present it as a finished client project. A case study is honest about being a self-run pilot, or it waits until a real client has agreed to be described.

Money, contracts, and the legal form of the business are path 09. Here you learn the engagement. Path 09's warnings still apply: do not sign a client's paper, or take their data, until you have had a lawyer look at the agreement and you understand the privacy note from path 07.

## What clients actually buy

They buy a reduced chance of a specific expensive mistake, or a reduced amount of a specific kind of work. They do not buy "an AI." Your discovery notes should name the work or the mistake in their words. Your metric should count that thing. A demo that writes fluent text and does not move the count is a lab exercise, and you already have those.

## How deep to go

- Keep the ladder from path 07 lab 01 in the proposal. Recommending a rule or a linear model when it wins is a consulting skill. Recommending a fine-tune every time is a sales habit.
- Write exclusions. The work you will not do protects both sides: no open-ended "make the company AI," no unlabeled data you must invent, no production hosting you are not staffed to wake up for.
- Separate a pilot (a few weeks, one workflow, one metric) from a platform (many teams, many models, ongoing operations). Early work is a pilot. Say so in the document.
- Claims follow measurements. If the golden set says 80%, the proposal does not say "accurate." It says what was measured, on which set, and what is still unchecked.

## Done with this path when

You have a wedge sentence, two honest write-ups, a discovery script, an SOW template you have filled once, a price you can explain, and a rehearsed packet you would be willing to send. The first real client is not a lab. The rehearsal is what keeps the first client from being your education.
